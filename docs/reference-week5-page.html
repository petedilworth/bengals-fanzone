What the result means

Cincinnati won with the run game this brief told it to avoid: 31 carries for 117 yards, a season high Bengals.com. Burrow had no turnovers, and Jordan Battle, flagged here for missed tackles, made both interceptions Bengals.com. The brief missed the real problem: Miami ran for 217 yards Heavy, and that run defence now faces Baltimore after the bye.

Main storylines

Ranked by how much each can change Sunday's result.

  1. 1

    Burrow's late-game turnovers

    He threw for 428 yards last week, with two interceptions. Both came when Jacksonville did not blitz AP. Since 2024 he leads the NFL in fourth-quarter and overtime turnovers TSN.

    Why it matters: yardage is not the issue. Decisions against four-man rushes are.

  2. 2

    Chase and Higgins: the Friday upgrade

    Both went from no practice early in the week to full practice Friday. Both are questionable Local 12. Chase still has to clear the concussion protocol.

    Non-obvious: the worst case is not both out. It is both in and limited, with a plan built around players who tire early.

  3. 3

    The run game is a symptom, not the disease

    The Bengals ran 15 times for 23 yards TSN. But Chase Brown caught all 11 of his targets WCPO. Short passes are doing the work of runs.

    Why it matters: Miami's run defence ranks sixth at 3.6 yards per carry CBS. Forcing carries pays poorly.

  4. 4

    A thin secondary that misses tackles

    Safety Kyle Dugger is out and Bryan Cook is questionable Local 12. Jordan Battle missed three tackles last week that let Jacksonville convert third-and-longs AP.

    Why it matters: Miami's weak passing game (Willis: 56.3%, one touchdown) will hide this. Better offences will not.

  5. 5

    Last game before the bye

    After the bye, Cincinnati plays at Baltimore on Oct. 25. A win over a winless team will hide every problem above.

    Why it matters: a star who re-injures himself Sunday costs the Baltimore game too.

What to watch for

In game order. Each call gets graded Monday at noon. "My read" means judgement, not reported fact.

Scorecard: 2 of 6 calls right, 2 partly, 1 wrong, 1 didn't happen.

  • About 11:30 am ET

    Read the inactives

    Partly

    Inactives come out 90 minutes before kickoff. Check Chase and Higgins first. If Chase plays, watch whether he runs full routes or gets a managed snap count.

    Both played and neither was held back: Chase took 89% of snaps and Higgins 69% A to Z Sports. The call was a checklist, not a prediction, so it cannot be graded higher.

  • First half

    Does Miami blitz Burrow?

    Partly

    Both interceptions came with no blitz. Coach Jeff Hafley ran the Packers' defence in 2024 and 2025 Dolphins notes. My read: expect disguised coverages and few blitzes.

    Miami sat in shell coverage, gave up short throws and never sacked Burrow Bengals.com. That fits the read, but no blitz count was published, so "few blitzes" is not proven.

  • Second half

    Pass rush against Willis

    Right

    Cincinnati has 14 sacks, tied for third. Dexter Lawrence has three CBS, but he missed Friday's practice. My read: if he plays, three or more sacks.

    Lawrence played 43 snaps A to Z Sports and Willis was sacked three times Heavy. Boye Mafe and Barrett Carter had the reported sacks, not Lawrence.

  • All game

    Burrow's dropback count

    Right

    He dropped back 58 times last week TSN. Near 40 suggests a lead. Near 55 suggests a close game or a failed run plan.

    Burrow threw 32 passes and was not sacked, so about 32 dropbacks Bengals.com. The low count matched a lead, as the call said it would.

  • Special teams

    Meyers does three jobs

    Wrong

    Last week he caught seven passes and returned eight kicks and punts Fantasy Footballers. If a star sits, his load climbs. My read: watch for a ball-security mistake.

    Both stars played, so Meyers got just 19 snaps A to Z Sports. He did not lose the ball; his late mistake was stepping out at the 1 on a punt return Yahoo.

  • Fourth quarter

    The margin with 10 minutes left

    Didn't happen

    Both losses were one-score games. My read: if it is within a score late, Burrow's turnover record matters more than any injury.

    Cincinnati led 28–10 with 10 minutes left, so the condition never came up theScore. Miami only got within a score with 45 seconds left, and Burrow had no turnovers.

Devil's Advocate

Where this brief may be wrong

  • The offence is fine. Burrow completed 72% of his passes last week CBS. The two losses were by 3 and 5 points. Late interceptions are high-variance. A small sample, not a flaw, may explain them.
  • Miami is better than 0–4. It held Kenneth Walker and Aaron Jones under 100 yards each and lost to Minnesota by five CBS. Seven points on the road is a lot to ask of a team that just lost at home.
  • "Questionable" may mean "playing." If both stars play at full strength, half of this page stops mattering.
Alternative view

The same game, from Miami

This is Miami's best chance to win. Cincinnati just lost at home, two stars are hobbled, a starting safety is out, and Burrow makes late mistakes.

Miami's defence is sixth against the run, and rookie linebacker Jacob Rodriguez is second in the NFL in tackles CBS. The plan: keep it close and let Burrow make the late mistake.

The catch: Willis has one touchdown pass all season. Miami cannot win a shootout.
